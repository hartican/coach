Do Less is evolving from a single-user home workout app into a family-ready coach that can handle very different people (you, Gina, your mum) without mixing their data or needs.  The core idea is: one solid “brain” that knows how to build sessions, plus separate “profiles” that describe who someone is, what they can safely do, and what success looks like for them.  Your current app is a mobile-first single-page PWA that stores everything in localStorage and already uses a block-based session builder, which is ideal for extracting into a reusable engine.[1][2]

At the center is a **session engine** that takes a profile, a small check-in, recent history, and context like time-of-day, then outputs a specific session: warm-up, main block, anti-core, optional finisher, and so on.  Around that sits a **shared exercise catalog** with rich tags (pattern, equipment, impact, skill level, contraindications, goals), so the engine can build very different plans from the same pool of movements.  Profiles tell the engine which tags are allowed, which are off-limits, and how fast to progress.[2]

Each person gets a **profile package**: a definition (goals, equipment, default session types), rules (progression, safety, adaptation), copy tone, and exercise filters.  Jack’s `jack_v1` profile keeps the current visible-abs, 20-minute strength-plus-core, ADHD-friendly design with fallback sessions that still count for streaks.  Gina’s `gina_postpartum_v1` profile focuses on gentle, symptom-gated, bands-and-mat strength for a 34-year-old two-months-postpartum mum with ADHD and Hashimoto’s.  Your mum’s `mum_active_aging_v1` profile centers on balance, confidence, and safe strength, with chair/wall support and slower progression.[2]

Under the hood, you add a **persistence layer** that combines local-first storage with lightweight Supabase.  Supabase gives you email-based logins, per-user tables (profiles, state, session completions, readiness logs), and row-level security so each person only sees their own data.  Local storage remains for offline use and fast loads, but is namespaced by profile ID to avoid the current “everything in one Jack bucket” situation.  Sync logic reconciles local and cloud events, but session plans are cheap to regenerate instead of merged.[1][2]

On top of that sits an **adaptation loop**: every completed or skipped session, plus each quick check-in, feeds into a profile-specific adaptation policy that only patches that user’s state.  For Gina, this might bias toward 6–10 minute “wins” if long sessions keep getting skipped, or unlock slightly harder band work when she reports low difficulty and zero symptoms.  For your mum, it might soften balance challenges until repeated success, then gradually remove supports.  Jack keeps his current streak and key-lift-driven progression, but that logic lives in his profile instead of in global code.[2]

A **phased rollout** keeps risk low. Phase 0 extracts the current Jack logic into a formal `jack_v1` profile and documents the engine contract without changing behavior.  Phase 1 separates “core” and “profiles” in code, and Phase 2 adds local multi-profile support with isolated state.  Phase 3 introduces Supabase auth and sync; Phase 4 adds the adaptation engine; Phase 5 fine-tunes Gina and mum based on real usage and a simple debug view showing what the engine chose and why.  You measure success when Jack’s experience is unchanged, Gina and your mum feel appropriately seen, and changes to one profile never leak into another.[1][2]
